        AirGradient Open Air

        Most of the outdoor sensors on this map are AirGradient. Open source, made to be repaired, and it becomes the trusted sensor for your area.

        • PM2.5, CO₂, TVOC, NOx, temperature and humidity
        • About USD 125 as a kit or USD 225 assembled (airgradient.com, September 2026)
        • About 2.5 weeks from Thailand, with duty prepaid at 19.2%
        • Turn on public sharing, or it is invisible to everyone else

        Act together

        Clean air is decided street by street.

        Over four months and 2,900 messages, about 240 residents worked out what actually gets burning stopped. Join them, and start where it works.

        Join the WhatsApp community ↗

        1. Your neighbour

          In person, not through a report. Ask what happens to their rubbish before you mention health, then talk about the children. Show the number on your phone, and bring a route with you: a compost bag costs about IDR 60k.

        2. The banjar

          What a klian needs is somewhere for the waste to go. Organic waste is about two-thirds of it, so start there: composting, Bank Sampah, TPS3R. Enforcement without a route only moves the fire.

        3. The regency (DLH)

          The environment office has the legal power. Bring your numbers and how you measured them, make numbered asks, and ask for an inspection while the burning is happening.

        4. SP4N-LAPOR

          File at the same time as the regency. It can be anonymous and gives you a ticket number to follow. Many separate complaints count for more than one letter.

        Two things the community learned the hard way. Say what the data shows, never who caused it: no sensor in Bali can name a source yet. And if you are a foreign resident, take advice before organising anything public, and let Indonesian residents lead it.
