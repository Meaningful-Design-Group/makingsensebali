# DfAM + product-design research (2026-06)

Why this exists: the v1–v6 enclosures kept being *injection-molded boxes printed
on an FDM machine* — lid + screws + brass inserts + bought gasket. That wastes the
medium. This is the research to design **additive-native** and to design like a
product designer, not a CAD operator. Five streams, cited. The actionable rules
live in the `outdoor-sensor-enclosure` skill; this is the evidence.

## 1. Print-in-place & zero-hardware mechanisms
Design snaps/flexures to **strain, not stress**. Reusable PETG cantilever: **L/h ≈ 10:1**, ~1 mm thick, deflect ~10% of length, **taper to 0.5× tip (K≈1.64)**, **root fillet ≥0.5× thickness**, **lead-in 15–30° / return 30–45°**, target **~1.8% working strain** (0.6× reuse-derated). **Print flexing features flat in XY** (Z-built ≈50% weaker). Print-in-place freedom clearance **0.4–0.5 mm** (≈1–1.25× extrusion width); hinge axle bores **cut at the 40° overhang, >60% circumference**, ~1 mm bridges. **Bayonet/twist-lock prints in place** and is the best no-hardware weatherproof closure. Living hinges (0.4–0.6 mm web) are **low-cycle only** on FDM. [Ticona/Celanese snap-fit manual](https://www.gotstogo.com/misc/engineering_info/Snap_Fitsres72dpi.PDF) · [Protolabs Network snap-fit](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/how-design-snap-fit-joints-3d-printing/) · [Lucky Resistor PETG print-in-place](https://luckyresistor.me/2023/02/07/the-hinges-and-its-secrets-for-perfect-petg-print) · [Formlabs snap-fit enclosures](https://formlabs.com/blog/designing-3d-printed-snap-fit-enclosures/)

## 2. Lattices / gyroid as functional skin (the signature move)
A gyroid has **no straight line of sight** — one **sheet (walled) gyroid** surface is simultaneously **structure + ventilation + sun-shade + rain-baffle**: air threads the helical channel, photons/droplets hit wall. Use the **sheet** (offset both sides open), not solid infill. Start **cell 6–8 mm, wall 1.0–1.5 mm, ~60–70% porosity**, pores **≥2 mm** (print open, shed water). **Grade porosity by function** (denser sun side, open intake/PM) — additive-only. **Caveat:** filament is IR-transparent, so a gyroid shades visible sun but the T/RH sensor still needs a **reflective foil liner + 2–3 mm air gap**. [nTop gyroid Periodic-Lattice](https://support.ntop.com/hc/en-us/articles/360035831653-How-to-model-a-gyroid-using-the-Periodic-Lattice-block) · [TPMS airflow resistivity (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2590048X23001164) · [sheet vs solid TPMS permeability CFD](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1350453323000796) · [Cave Pearl / Hackaday IR caveat](https://hackaday.com/2022/02/04/3d-printed-radiation-shields-get-put-to-the-test/) · [exposed gyroid in slicer, Prusa forum](https://forum.prusa3d.com/forum/english-forum-general-discussion-announcements-and-releases/exposed-gyroid-infill/)

**Proven in our toolchain:** sheet gyroid via `skimage.measure.marching_cubes(|g| − t)` → `trimesh` → STL, with `t` graded spatially for a solid frame + open centre. See `v7/gyroid_test*` and `v7/v7_gyroid_vent_panel*`.

## 3. Monocoque / orientation-as-formgiver
**Pick the no-support orientation first; let it be the form.** Print upright, dome/cone up → the shell **sheds rain because of its shape**. No flat top (water wicks layer lines); **teardrop every horizontal hole**; **chamfer downward edges, fillet seen edges**; stiffness from **curvature/flutes**, not wall mass; layer lines horizontal → shed sideways; Z-seam off weather faces, in a designed gutter; nautiloid/conical shells are self-supporting + stiff + elegant. [Prusa vase-mode / layers](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/layers-and-perimeters_1748) · [Formlabs watertight + nautiloid](https://formlabs.com/blog/watertight-3d-printing/) · [Pollen AM teardrop holes](https://www.pollen.am/design_guidelines_holes/) · [anisotropy](https://www.ymolding.com/isotropic-vs-anisotropic-why-3d-print-direction-affects-part-strength/)

## 4. Product-design craft (box → product)
A **radius system** (R1/R2/R4…) on every edge; **1–2° draft**; **lifted base/feet**; a **0.5–1 mm shadow-gap reveal** at the lid line; **painted fuzzy skin** on the body (hides layer lines + seam, matte soft-touch), smooth mating faces; **monotonic + ironed** flats; **openings on a shared grid**; **tight CMF** (one matte body + one accent); **system over one-off** so Basic+Plus read as a family. Beauty is a consequence of solving function cleanly under a small rule set. Exemplars: Teenage Engineering (restraint), Prusa CORE One (structure-as-skin), Bambu AMS (function→form), Gridfinity/Framework (design system). [Fuzzy skin (Prusa)](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/fuzzy-skin_246186) · [Seam/scarf (Prusa)](https://help.prusa3d.com/article/seam-position_151069) · [TE design language](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/teenage-engineering) · [Gridfinity spec](https://gridfinity.xyz/specification/) · [Formlabs enclosure walkthrough](https://formlabs.com/blog/designing-3d-printed-snap-fit-enclosures/)

## 5. Printed weatherproofing (breathe, don't seal)
**Roof, don't seal:** overhanging eave, **≥8–10 mm skirt overlap**, **30–45° shed**, parting line down-and-out; **drip edge** = sharp un-filleted lip + 1–2 mm groove; **labyrinth tongue-and-groove, groove female-DOWN** (can't fill), tongue 1.2–1.6 mm, +0.2–0.25 mm/side, engagement 3–5 mm; **downward louvres ≤45°, slots ≥1.5–2 mm**; watertight wall = **4 PETG perimeters, 0.15 mm, +5–10 °C, +5–10% flow, LA off**. **Still bought, honestly:** a fine insect-mesh scrap (FDM can't print mosquito apertures — a printed infill-grille does ~1–2 mm holes), a cable gland (the #1 leak path), an ePTFE vent only for IP65+. Don't chase submersion; target **IP54-by-geometry + breathe-and-drain**. [Prusa watertight](https://blog.prusa3d.com/watertight-3d-printing-pt1-vases-cups-and-other-open-models_48949/) · [Sovol outdoor enclosure](https://www.sovol3d.com/blogs/news/3d-printed-electronics-enclosure-for-smart-gardening-what-actually-matters-outdoors) · [ProgressTH water-resistant tests](https://hackaday.io/project/174011-water-resistant-3d-printed-enclosures) · [printed mesh limits, Prusa forum](https://forum.prusa3d.com/forum/english-forum-original-prusa-i3-mk4s-how-do-i-print-this/fine-mesh-for-mosquito-screen-not-working/)

## The additive-native enclosure that follows (v7 DNA)
A **monocoque, upright-printed, self-supporting shell** whose sloped form sheds rain; a **sheet-gyroid breathable skin** over the sensor zone (structure + vent + shade in one, foil-lined + air-gapped for the BME680); a **print-in-place / snap closure — zero hardware**; weatherproofing by **eave + drip + labyrinth + downward louvres**, not gaskets; **product-design craft** (radius system, draft, fuzzy skin, shadow-gap, lifted base, one CMF) so Basic + Plus are a family. That is the build v7 executes.
